Part 1: The conversation

A
Amara — Young job seekerI use my phone every day. I chat, watch videos and follow social media. But I still feel that opportunities pass me by. What am I doing wrong?
D
Mr. Daniel — Career mentorUsing a phone is not the same as using it with purpose. Your phone can entertain you, but it can also help you learn, build skills, meet people, apply for jobs and create evidence of what you can do.
A
AmaraSo, is entertainment bad?
D
Mr. DanielNo. Entertainment can be healthy rest, creativity and even a career. The question is balance: after your online time, are you more skilled, more connected, more prepared or closer to an opportunity?
“Am I using the internet to advance my future, or am I only entertaining myself?”

Part 4: Internet is a career asset

A
AmaraData is expensive. How should I use it wisely?
D
Mr. DanielTreat data like an investment. Reserve it for job alerts, applications, learning, email, professional networking and interviews. Download large files on Wi‑Fi when possible, and reduce automatic video downloads.
A
AmaraWhat if several young people live in one house?
D
Mr. DanielIf three or more people live together, a shared home Wi‑Fi router or outdoor broadband unit can reduce costs. But first confirm the network is strong at the exact address. Agree contributions and protect productive use—interviews, classes and remote work should come before heavy streaming or game downloads.

Smart shared-Wi‑Fi rules

  • Share the subscription cost fairly and renew it on time.
  • Keep the Wi‑Fi password within the household.
  • Prioritise job interviews, assessments, online classes and remote work.
  • Keep backup mobile data or a hotspot for important moments.

Part 5: Build a useful online presence

What should my online presence say about me?
It should show that you are serious, respectful, developing useful skills and ready for opportunities. Start with a professional email address, a complete LinkedIn profile, a clear profile photo and accurate information about your education, projects, work, volunteering and skills.
What can I post if I have little work experience?
Share evidence of progress: a completed course, a useful lesson from a webinar, a school or volunteer project, a spreadsheet you created, a presentation, an event you supported, or a short reflection on an industry topic. Be truthful—do not invent experience.
How can I use social media without wasting all my time?
Follow employers, professional bodies, educators and people working in your target field. Set a daily time limit for entertainment. Make one useful action part of each online session: apply, learn, connect, save a vacancy, update your CV or write a thoughtful comment.
Which apps should I keep ready?
WhatsApp, email, LinkedIn, Google Drive, a PDF reader, Google Docs or Microsoft Word, a browser, Zoom or Teams, a calendar and a cloud-backup app are a strong starting toolkit.

Your 7-day digital progress challenge

Do one practical action each day. Small, consistent steps build readiness.

  • Day 1: Create or improve your professional email address.
  • Day 2: Update your CV and save it as a PDF.
  • Day 3: Create or improve your LinkedIn profile.
  • Day 4: Turn on job alerts for roles you want.
  • Day 5: Organise your certificates and documents in cloud storage.
  • Day 6: Complete one short lesson or career-related course activity.
  • Day 7: Apply for one suitable opportunity or make one useful professional connection.
